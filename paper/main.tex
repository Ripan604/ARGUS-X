\begin{abstract}
ARGUS NEO is an inspectable research prototype for sequential acoustic interrogation of a panel with one dominant scatterer. It maintains a spatial posterior and separate metrology uncertainty, including correlated velocity and timing estimates; selects finite source--receiver--waveform candidates; and supports diagnosis, direct-reference calibration, verification, and abstention. The default controller uses labelled heuristic utilities. An optional controller compares calibration and diagnosis using expected squared localization error plus acquisition cost in square metres, with Gaussian observation branches over at most two measurements. Quality, accumulated channel reliability, and out-of-distribution checks precede structural fusion. Evidence exports support integrity verification against an independently retained checkpoint, not measurement authentication. In a historical September synthetic check of six paired seeds per condition and six measurements per case, nominal mean errors were 43.90 mm for the legacy controller and 12.58 mm for the optional controller; under velocity, attenuation, and noise mismatch they were 150.22 and 203.78 mm. Both paired bootstrap advantage intervals included zero. These results do not validate the changed October source. The optional controller remains off by default. The October revision adds bounded acquisition/import contracts, network controls, and scientific regression cases. The implementation does not establish physical accuracy, a general joint posterior, commercial superiority, or legal patentability.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Nondestructive evaluation (NDE) is usually presented as a mapping from a measurement to a decision: acquire a waveform, compute features, and classify or localize damage. That formulation leaves the measurement geometry fixed. Yet the source position, receiver position, waveform, frequency band, amplitude, and duration determine which aspects of an internal structure are observable. When measurements have different costs, choosing the next experiment can be as important as interpreting the previous one.

ARGUS addresses the sequential question
\begin{equation}
 e_{t+1}^{\star}=\arg\max_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{t}}
 \left[\mathcal{I}_t(e)-\mathcal{C}_t(e)\right],
 \label{eq:intro-objective}
\end{equation}
where $e$ is a physically executable experiment and $\mathcal{E}_{t}$ is a state-dependent candidate set. After observing response $y_{t+1}$, ARGUS performs a recursive Bayesian update, regenerates candidates from the new posterior, and repeats. This is related to Bayesian experimental design and active data selection \cite{chaloner1995,mackay1992}, but instantiated as a live, spatial, source--receiver interrogation loop.

The prototype targets an opaque rectangular panel with one dominant hidden anomaly (cavity, loose region, delamination, or dense inclusion). Its central distinction from a one-shot classifier is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:comparison}. It is designed to run without hardware through a seeded physics-inspired simulator. Physical recordings are accepted for history and analysis, but arrival inference additionally requires supported excitation-relative timing and a declared validated arrival adapter.

The contributions of this implementation are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item an observe--infer--plan--act loop joining simulation or acquisition to block-structured spatial and metrology estimates, retaining velocity--timing covariance rather than claiming a general joint posterior;
    \item a dual-control policy that switches among diagnostic, calibration, verification, and abstention actions and evaluates counterfactual observation distributions using risk, worst-rival separation, information, movement, energy, redundancy, trust, and safety terms;
    \item selection among waveform families and source--receiver geometries, with two live analytical/physics-signature fidelity levels; the legacy route lookahead is a geometric proxy, while the optional risk controller branches on observations;
    \item likelihood tempering, online model-discrepancy correction, OOD indicators, calibration diagnostics, and explicit verify/abstain states, without claiming field-calibrated probabilities;
    \item an auditable research stack comprising resumable jobs, paired benchmarks, ablations, fault injection, truth-sealed replay, hash-chained evidence export, phone-camera guidance, and a reconnecting edge node;
    \item tested endpoints, numerical and safety regression tests, and a production frontend build; the dated audit report records the current verification evidence.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Revision Scope: 7 October 2026}
The following architectural equations describe modelling intent unless explicitly identified as executed. The runtime is block-structured, not a fully coupled structural--metrology posterior. Learned-surrogate and imported-response-bank fidelity levels are not validated live-controller implementations. Multimodal sensing, robotics, multi-defect localization, and reliable defect type, depth, or severity inference are outside the demonstrated scope. The terms ``dual control'' and ``Bayes risk'' in the legacy formulation denote engineered proxies; the distinct, optional physical-loss controller below is the implemented finite-horizon Bayesian design approximation. Healthy/damage/unknown scores and trust values are also heuristic, not certified probabilities.

A direct-path observation satisfies $y=d/v+b+\epsilon$, with propagation state $\theta=(v,b)$ and covariance $C$. Its Jacobian is $h=(-d/v^2,1)$. The Gaussian covariance update is implemented in Joseph form,
\begin{align}
 C^+&=(I-Kh)C(I-Kh)^T+KRK^T,\\
 K&=Ch^T(hCh^T+R)^{-1}.
\end{align}
A single delay supplies rank-one information; different reference path lengths are necessary to separate velocity from timing. Absolute pose requires independently measured pose errors and uncertainty. Waveform quality alone is not treated as separate evidence for both transmitter and receiver coupling.

For spatial regions with probabilities $p_j$, centres $x_j$ in metres and unresolved within-region variances $s_j^2$, the optional controller uses
\begin{equation}
 r(p)=\sum_jp_j(\|x_j\|^2+s_j^2)-\left\|\sum_jp_jx_j\right\|^2.
\end{equation}
All posterior mass contributes. Calibration and diagnosis share this loss and a declared cost conversion. Within a finite beam of candidate actions, the two-step objective is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 Q(a)=c(a)+\E_{y\mid a}\min\big\{r(p_y),\\
 \min_b[c(b\mid a)+\E_{z\mid y,a,b}r(p_{y,z})]\big\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Conditioning the second observation includes the shared velocity--timing cross-covariance. The second-step options are diagnostics or stopping; this is not unrestricted multi-step optimal control. Independent measurement and pose noise, linearized propagation, region coarsening, and conditional within-region position independence remain assumptions. Gauss--Hermite orders 16 and 32 provide a reported numerical sensitivity diagnostic, not a rigorous integration error bound. Planning's arrival model and inference's generalized matched-filter likelihood remain different approximations.

OOD assessment now precedes fusion. Rejected or stale verification labels cannot satisfy the stopping gate. A successful direct reference validates neither the scattered-wave model nor field coverage, and fitted calibration residuals no longer populate the structural conformal reference pool. Integrity verification checks stored acquisition content; a separately retained ledger head and count are needed to detect whole-history replacement. Checksums alone do not establish acquisition authenticity.

The candidate technical contribution is deliberately narrow. Guided-wave NDE, Bayesian localization, Bayesian sensor placement, active learning, dual control, matched filtering, and active digital-twin calibration all predate this work \cite{rose2014,flynn2010,cantero2019,turin1960,fei2025patent}. The implementation proposed for examination couples live rival-mode discrimination, structural--metrology action arbitration, constrained candidate selection, gated evidence, and verifiable decision history. Neither technical novelty nor legal patentability is established by this manuscript.

\section{Related Work and Design Position}
\subsection{Guided-Wave and Vibration-Based Inspection}
Elastic waves can interrogate plate-like structures over areas larger than a probe footprint, while damage modifies propagation through scattering, attenuation, mode conversion, and local resonance \cite{rose2014}. Practical guided-wave interpretation is complicated by dispersion, anisotropy, boundaries, coupling variability, and environmental drift. Open Guided Waves provides controlled CFRP measurements for comparative algorithm research \cite{moll2019}, illustrating both the value and the complexity of measured wavefields.

ARGUS does not claim that its compact simulator is a high-fidelity Lamb-wave or finite-element model. It uses path length, propagation delay, attenuation, signed defect reflection, frequency-sensitive gain, and damped ringing to create an interpretable inverse problem that can exercise the complete adaptive loop. The real-device path is intentionally decoupled from this simulator.

\subsection{Bayesian Design and Active Measurement}
Bayesian experimental design selects an experiment according to expected utility under current uncertainty \cite{chaloner1995}; information-based active selection chooses data expected to be informative about hypotheses or predictions \cite{mackay1992}. In structural health monitoring, Flynn and Todd formulated optimal actuator/sensor placement using Bayes risk and demonstrated active guided-wave examples \cite{flynn2010}. Much of that literature optimizes a fixed network or configuration. ARGUS instead replans a single next, executable experiment after every observation and incorporates movement, excitation, and redundancy costs.

Exact expected information gain marginalizes future observations for each candidate and depends on the generative model. The default fast path uses a bounded counterfactual-disagreement proxy under its legacy ``expected information gain'' name. The optional nuisance-arrival extension instead numerically integrates information for a declared regional scalar model; neither calculation guarantees global optimality or information gain under real wavefields.

\subsection{Open Experimental Evidence}
The LMSD 2021 dataset contains measured frequency-response functions (FRFs) from a $600\times600\times4$ mm cross-ply CFRP plate, a $12\times12$ coordinate grid, seven accelerometer/hammer nodes, a healthy baseline, and six added-mass scenarios \cite{dwek2024dataset}. It supplies real multistatic responses and known mass locations, but not cavities, delaminations, or adaptively selected trials. ARGUS uses it to test data ingestion and feature transfer, not to imply physical validation of every supported defect type.

\subsection{Crowded Prior Art and Commercial Position}
The field is crowded at both the apparatus and inference levels. Embedded Lamb-wave arrays, phased focusing, baseline comparison, multi-path prediction, Bayesian damage localization, and Bayesian actuator/sensor placement are established \cite{giurgiutiu2005patent,hall2018patent,boeing2025patent,flynn2010,cantero2019}. Autonomous metrology programs also combine physics-informed learning, active experiment design, and adaptive calibration \cite{nist2025sams}; EP~4~592~907~A1 describes recursive Bayesian digital-twin calibration and active instrument-setting adjustments \cite{fei2025patent}. Accordingly, neither ``AI with ultrasound,'' ``Bayesian guided waves,'' ``choose another measurement,'' nor ``self-calibration'' is asserted as novel.

\section{Problem Formulation}
Let the panel occupy normalized coordinates $\Omega=[0,1]^2$. Physical distance is evaluated after the transformation
\begin{equation}
 \vect{p}_{\mathrm{m}}=\mathrm{diag}(W,H)\vect{p},
 \label{eq:physical-map}
\end{equation}
where the default width and height are $W=0.60$ m and $H=0.40$ m. The latent defect state is
\begin{equation}
 z=(x,y,r_x,r_y,q,T),
\end{equation}
containing center, elliptical radii, severity $q\in[0,1]$, and type $T$. The current prototype updates only location $(x,y)$ on a $G\times G$ grid, conditional on one dominant scatterer. Simulator randomization varies the other attributes, but the displayed size, severity, and type values remain unchanged priors; there is no measured attribute likelihood or defect-existence model.

An experiment is
\begin{equation}
 e=(\vect{s},\vect{r},f_0,f_1,a,d,w),
\end{equation}
where $\vect{s}$ and $\vect{r}$ are normalized source and receiver coordinates, $f_0,f_1$ are band endpoints, $a$ is normalized amplitude, $d$ is duration, and $w$ is a member of the finite waveform library. The historical baseline used impulse, sine, and chirp families. Given observation history $\mathcal{D}_t=\{(e_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^{t}$, the belief grid is
\begin{equation}
 P_t(j)=P(z_{xy}=j\mid\mathcal{D}_t),\qquad
 \sum_{j=1}^{G^2}P_t(j)=1.
\end{equation}
The initial belief is uniform. Each observation supplies a likelihood $L_{t+1}(j)$ and
\begin{equation}
 P_{t+1}(j)=\frac{P_t(j)\max[L_{t+1}(j),\eps]^{\beta_t}}
 {\sum_kP_t(k)\max[L_{t+1}(k),\eps]^{\beta_t}},\quad \eps=10^{-12}.
 \label{eq:bayes}
\end{equation}
Here $\beta_t\in[0,1]$ is the executed evidence gate, with zero for rejected, unreliable, OOD-blocked, or calibration-only structural evidence. Probability normalization uses scaled/log weights and preserves supported zero/nonzero distinctions. The normalized entropy is
\begin{equation}
 \Hn(P)=-\frac{\sum_{j:P(j)>0}P(j)\log_2(P(j))}{\log_2(G^2)}\in[0,1].
 \label{eq:entropy}
\end{equation}

\section{Physics-Inspired Forward Model}
\subsection{Excitation}
For $N=\lfloor d f_s\rceil$ samples at default sample rate $f_s=16$ kHz, the excitation occupies the first
\begin{equation}
 N_b=\left\lfloor f_s\min(10\,\mathrm{ms},0.22d)\right\rceil
\end{equation}
samples. A Tukey window with shape parameter 0.45 suppresses burst discontinuities \cite{harris1978}. A sine uses $\sin(2\pi f_0t)$; a chirp sweeps linearly from $f_0$ to $f_1$; and the impulse option uses a Ricker-like pulse
\begin{align}
 u(t)&=a[1-2\phi(t)^2]\exp[-\phi(t)^2],\\
 \phi(t)&=\frac{\pi(t-0.28d_b)}{\max(f_c^{-1},0.22\,\mathrm{ms})}.
\end{align}

\subsection{Direct and Scattered Responses}
Let $d_{sr}$ denote physical source--receiver distance and let $d_{sz},d_{zr}$ denote the two legs through the defect. The response is
\begin{equation}
 y(t)=A_0u(t-\tau_0)+A_zu(t-\tau_z)+r_z(t)+n(t)+b(t),
 \label{eq:forward}
\end{equation}
with fractional delays obtained by linear interpolation. For wave velocity $c$, attenuation $\alpha$, and system delay $t_0$,
\begin{align}
 \tau_0 &= t_0+d_{sr}/c,& A_0&=\frac{e^{-\alpha d_{sr}}}{1+4d_{sr}},\\
 \tau_z &= t_0+(d_{sz}+d_{zr})/c.&
\end{align}
The defect resonance and scattered gain are
\begin{align}
 f_z &= f_{\mathrm{mat}}(1.05-0.32q),\\
 R_f &=0.72+0.72\exp\!\left[-\frac{1}{2}
 \left(\frac{f_c-f_z}{1250}\right)^2\right],\\
 A_z &=0.43\rho_Tq\frac{\sqrt{r_xr_y}}{0.085}
 \frac{e^{-\alpha(d_{sz}+d_{zr})}}{1+5(d_{sz}+d_{zr})}R_f,
 \label{eq:scatter}
\end{align}
where $\rho_T=1.00,0.72,0.86,-0.64$ for cavity, loose region, delamination, and dense inclusion, respectively. The negative dense-inclusion coefficient creates a phase inversion. Starting at $\tau_z$,
\begin{equation}
 r_z(t)=0.11 A_z a\sin[2\pi f_r(t-\tau_z)]e^{-\beta(t-\tau_z)},
\end{equation}
where $f_r=f_z$ except for dense inclusions, for which $f_r=1.12f_z$. Noise $n(t)$ is Gaussian; $b(t)$ is a cumulative low-frequency random drift. The default material values are $c=180$ m/s, $\alpha=1.65$ m$^{-1}$, $f_{\mathrm{mat}}=3100$ Hz, $\beta=95$ s$^{-1}$, $t_0=0.8$ ms, and noise standard deviation 0.007.

This model creates a fast, interpretable inverse problem for candidate evaluation. It omits dispersion branches, anisotropic stiffness, boundary-mode conversion, transducer electromechanics, and spatially varying coupling. Geometry can remain ambiguous, and simulation accuracy must not be confused with field accuracy.

\section{Signal Interpretation and Recursive Belief}
\subsection{Preprocessing and Features}
The pipeline rejects nonfinite or shorter-than-eight-sample signals, removes the mean, and optionally applies a fourth-order zero-phase Butterworth bandpass from 250 Hz to $\min(7000,0.96f_s/2)$ Hz. Peak normalization is optional and disabled for feature extraction so amplitude remains informative. A Hann-windowed real FFT produces a unit-sum spectral energy distribution. Welch PSD, a short-time spectrogram, and a Hilbert envelope support inspection \cite{welch1967,oppenheim1999}.

Table~\ref{tab:features} lists the 15 learned forward-response targets. The runtime inspector additionally returns a robust noise estimate. Low-, mid-, and high-band fractions span 250--1500, 1500--3500, and 3500--7000 Hz (clipped by Nyquist). Spectral entropy is normalized by the log of the number of FFT bins. Decay time is the first post-envelope-peak sample below $1/e$ of the peak. The noise scale is $1.4826$ times the median absolute deviation (MAD) in the first 10\% of the record.

\subsection{Matched-Filter Spatial Likelihood}
The inference engine subtracts the predicted healthy direct response, giving residual $v=y-\hat y_0$. It correlates $v$ with the excitation using an FFT implementation, retains nonnegative lags, takes absolute magnitude, and applies a seven-sample maximum filter. Correlation is the classical matched-filter operation for a known waveform in noise \cite{turin1960}.

For every grid-cell center $j$, the model predicts the bistatic delay
\begin{equation}
 \hat\tau_j=t_0+\frac{d(\vect{s},\vect{z}_j)+d(\vect{z}_j,\vect{r})}{c}.
 \label{eq:bistatic}
\end{equation}
The correlation value at the nearest nonnegative lag is assigned to that cell, smoothed spatially by a Gaussian with $\sigma=0.55$ cells, and robustly scaled between its 8th and 98th percentiles to $\tilde C_j\in[0,1]$. The likelihood is
\begin{align}
 L(j)&\propto \exp(T_{\mathrm{eff}}\tilde C_j),\\
 T_{\mathrm{eff}}&=4.8\,\mathrm{clip}\!\left(
 \frac{\mathrm{SNR}-0.75}{1.8},0.30,1\right),
 \label{eq:likelihood}
\end{align}
where SNR is residual RMS divided by residual MAD scale. Scaling $L$ to have mean one makes the Bayesian update numerically convenient without altering Eq.~\eqref{eq:bayes}.

\subsection{Estimation, Confidence, and Termination}
ARGUS reports both the maximum-a-posteriori (MAP) grid center and posterior mean. The $2\times2$ covariance is the posterior-weighted covariance of cell coordinates and yields a confidence ellipse. Let $m_{\mathrm{local}}$ be posterior mass in a square neighborhood of radius $\max(1,\lfloor G/16\rfloor)$ around the MAP cell. The displayed confidence is the bounded heuristic
\begin{equation}
 Q=\mathrm{clip}\{0.55m_{\mathrm{local}}+0.45[1-\Hn(P)],0,1\}.
 \label{eq:confidence}
\end{equation}
The current stopping engine distinguishes budget exhaustion, OOD abstention, low modeled value, and a verified concentration stop. The latter requires the latest accepted verification to pass channel, OOD, diversity, and prior-location consistency checks, together with a configured concentration/area, entropy/value, or heuristic-risk criterion. Confidence or entropy alone cannot authorize a confident stop. A budget stop establishes no correctness. The historical baseline study used a ten-measurement budget; the default runtime budget is twelve.

\section{Adaptive Physical Experiment Planner}
\subsection{Candidate Construction}
The baseline generator mixes eight perimeter locations with up to eight probability-aware, spatially separated cell representatives as candidate sources. Receivers are the four perimeter corners plus the center. It cycles through bands 1.2--3.0, 2.2--4.4, and 3.4--6.2 kHz, with chirp, impulse, and chirp waveforms, amplitudes 0.42, 0.48, or 0.54, and 0.12 s duration. Near-coincident pairs are redirected and parameter duplicates removed, targeting the default pool of 48 candidates. The fast NEO path expands this baseline with finite waveform variants before applying constraints.

\subsection{Counterfactual Hypothesis Disagreement}
Let $\mathcal{K}$ contain up to $K=24$ probability-aware spatial representatives in the baseline scorer, with represented posterior mass $M=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{K}}P(i)$ and normalized weights $w_i=P(i)/M$. The fast NEO feature path uses up to five representatives; regional arrival modes preserve all mass through regional assignments. For candidate $e$, the baseline forward signature is
\begin{equation}
 \vect{g}_i(e)=\left[\tau_i,\log(A_i+10^{-8}),\sin\varphi_i,\cos\varphi_i\right],
 \quad \varphi_i=2\pi f_c\tau_i,
 \end{equation}
where $A_i=0.65e^{-\alpha d_i}/(1+5d_i)$. Delay and log-gain dimensions are divided by 0.18 ms and 0.55, respectively, while sine/cosine encode circular phase without wrap discontinuities. With scaled signature $\tilde{\vect g}$, pairwise distinguishability and disagreement are
\begin{align}
 D_{ij}(e)&=1-\exp\left[-\tfrac12\|\tilde{\vect g}_i(e)-\tilde{\vect g}_j(e)\|_2^2\right],\\
 D(e)&=\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{K}}w_iw_jD_{ij}(e).
 \label{eq:disagreement}
\end{align}
The bounded information-gain proxy is
\begin{equation}
 I(e)=H(P)M D(e),
 \label{eq:eig}
\end{equation}
in bits. It grows with current uncertainty, posterior mass represented by the leading hypotheses, and predicted separability. It is not the expected value of Eq.~\eqref{eq:entropy} under a calibrated observation distribution.

Candidate coverage is
\begin{equation}
 U(e)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{K}}w_i\exp[-2.2\min(d_{si},d_{ir})].
\end{equation}
The implemented physical cost is
\begin{equation}
 C(e)=0.52a^2\frac{d}{0.12}+0.48\frac{\|\vect{s}-\vect{s}_{t}\|_2}{\sqrt2},
 \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
with zero motion term for the first experiment. Redundancy is the closest match to any previous experiment:
\begin{equation}
 R(e)=\max_{h<t}\exp[-6\|\vect{s}-\vect{s}_h\|_2]
 \exp[-2|f_c-f_{c,h}|/3000].
 \label{eq:repeat}
\end{equation}
The final score is
\begin{equation}
 S(e)=I(e)+0.35D(e)+0.22U(e)-0.16C(e)-0.22R(e).
 \label{eq:planner}
\end{equation}
The highest-scoring candidate is selected. The interface exposes all terms for the top five candidates, preventing the planner from becoming an opaque recommendation engine.

\subsection{Programmatic Explanation}
The selected recommendation receives a deterministic rationale. High disagreement produces a competing-regions explanation; broad entropy produces a coverage explanation; otherwise the system describes local ambiguity resolution. If repetition is below 0.25 after prior measurements, the explanation notes that the geometry and frequency avoid recent experiments. \section{Trust-Gated Counterfactual Dual Control}
\subsection{Block-Structured Spatial and Metrology State}
The original spatial grid is retained for location inference, while NEO stores a separate metrology block. The executed state can be written as
\begin{equation}
 B_t=\{P_t(x,y),\hat{\vect\eta}_t,C_t,\rho_t,o_t\},
 \label{eq:joint-state}
\end{equation}
where $\vect\eta$ contains wave velocity, attenuation, timing offset, noise scale, gain, source/receiver coupling, and source/receiver pose error. $C_t$ retains velocity--timing covariance; other metrology quantities remain approximate marginal moments. This is not a stored hierarchical joint structural--metrology posterior. A seeded correlated particle-sampling helper exists and is tested, but the live controller does not call it. Size, severity, and type remain fixed priors.

Accepted diagnostic evidence updates location and may update a passive noise estimate. Reference calibration updates only supported metrology parameters; it never supplies structural evidence. Estimated velocity, timing, and noise means are synchronized into inference without changing hidden acquisition physics. Signal quality uses amplitude, clipping, finite-sample validity, repeat consistency, and placement proxies to produce $w_t\in[0,1]$. The recursive structural update is
\begin{equation}
 P_{t+1}(j)\propto P_t(j)
 \max\{L(y_{t+1}\mid j,e_{t+1},\hat{\vect\eta}_t),\eps\}^{\beta_t},
 \label{eq:tempered-update}
\end{equation}
where accepted quality has base weight $\max(w_t,w_{\min})$. A degraded channel and an OOD caution each multiply that weight by 0.35; an unreliable channel, rejected waveform, OOD abstention, or reference-only action sets the structural exponent to zero. There is no additional executed $\rho_t/T_t$ multiplier. Model trust affects diagnostics, planning, and reported confidence. Rejected or unsupported observations cannot sharpen belief.

\subsection{Dual Control: Diagnose or Calibrate}
An action may interrogate the object or reduce uncertainty in the measuring process itself. Let $U_s$ denote structural uncertainty, $U_m$ combined metrology uncertainty, $\rho$ model trust, and $o$ the OOD score. NEO compares
\begin{align}
 V_{\mathrm{cal}}&=\left[U_m(0.70+0.30(1-\rho))+0.35o\right]d_n,\\
 V_{\mathrm{diag}}&=\hat I(e)U_s\rho,
 \label{eq:dual-control}
\end{align}
with diminishing-return factor $d_n=(1+0.65n_{\mathrm{cal}})^{-1}$. Calibration is selected when $V_{\mathrm{cal}}-V_{\mathrm{diag}}>0.05$, or when an explicit threshold/OOD rule requires it. The dominant nuisance term maps to an executable direct-path, coupling-repeat, phone-pose, microphone-level, or frequency-sweep action. Otherwise NEO selects diagnostic, exploration, or pre-stop verification. This is a transparent heuristic dual controller whose components and switching margin are returned by the API.

\subsection{Counterfactual Observation Distributions and Bayes Risk}
For the leading hypotheses $h_i$, the fast controller supplies compact feature predictions and assumed variances. Standardized mean separation and predictive variance yield average and worst-pair proxies. The legacy risk index is explicitly parameterized:
\begin{equation}
 R_t=12p_{\mathrm{miss}}+3p_{\mathrm{false\ alarm}},
 \quad p_{\mathrm{miss}}=\mathrm{clip}(1-c_t+0.45o_t),
 \label{eq:risk}
\end{equation}
The symbols $p_{\mathrm{miss}}$ and $p_{\mathrm{false\ alarm}}$ are heuristic indices, not fitted event probabilities. Predicted reduction is a bounded function of information, separation, and trust. The default multiobjective utility is
\begin{align}
 J(e)=&\,I(e)+0.35D(e)+0.55\Delta R(e)\nonumber\\
 &+0.65V_{\mathrm{cal}}(e)+0.30\rho+\beta U(e)\nonumber\\
 &-0.16C(e)-0.22R(e)-0.08T(e)-0.08E(e),
 \label{eq:neo-utility}
\end{align}
where $\beta$ depends on exploration state. Alternative objectives expose information gain, Bayes risk, worst-case ambiguity, and information per physical cost. False-negative, false-positive, escalation, abstention, movement, time, and excitation losses are configuration values; they are research utilities, not certified maintenance economics.

\subsection{Formal TG-CDI Policy}
The compact implementation above is summarized by the following policy abstraction. Let $\mathcal H_t=\{h_1,\ldots,h_K\}$ contain spatially separated posterior representatives. At either live model level $\ell\in\{0,1\}$, the fast controller uses a compact Gaussian feature approximation
\begin{equation}
 q^{(\ell)}_{i,e}(y)\approx
 \mathcal N\!\left(\mu^{(\ell)}_{i,e},\Sigma^{(\ell)}_{i,e}\right),
 \label{eq:rival-predictive}
\end{equation}
whose moments depend on geometry, frequency, and selected metrology moments. Amplitude, duration, and code effects enter separate waveform/cost heuristics rather than a validated full-waveform predictive distribution. Separation combines a bounded symmetric-KL transform, Bhattacharyya separation, and predictive-variance proxies. The legacy-named ``Jensen--Shannon'' score is $1-\exp(-\mathrm{SKL}/4)$, not actual JS divergence. The hard-rival term
\begin{equation}
 S_{\min}(e)=\min_{i\ne j}S\!\left(q^{(\ell)}_{i,e},q^{(\ell)}_{j,e}\right)
 \label{eq:hard-rival}
\end{equation}
penalizes a candidate whose weakest represented pair has low predicted separation; it does not guarantee identification of all true rival locations.

A more general prospective policy could compare diagnostic expected loss reduction $G_z$ with metrology information gain $G_\eta$ using
\begin{align}
 U_D(e)&=\rho_t\!\left[w_zG_z+w_aS_{\mathrm{avg}}+w_mS_{\min}\right]-C(e),\nonumber\\
 U_C(e)&=w_\eta G_\eta+w_\rho\E[\Delta\rho_t\mid e]-C(e),
 \label{eq:tgcdi-utility}
\end{align}
These prospective utilities are not the executed fast-controller calculation. The fast controller first chooses an action class by Eq.~\eqref{eq:dual-control}, then ranks feasible candidates using Eq.~\eqref{eq:neo-utility}. An unrestricted common-value competition across all action classes would instead be
\begin{equation}
 (a_t,e_t)^*=\arg\max_{(a,e)\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{safe}}}
 \{U_D,U_C,U_V,U_E,U_{\mathrm{abstain}}\}.
 \label{eq:tgcdi-policy}
\end{equation}
This unrestricted optimization is not implemented. The optional physical-loss controller compares calibration and diagnosis within its finite beam and observation horizon; a diagnostic can be labelled verification when configured concentration conditions hold. Unsupported planning enters abstention. Actual structural fusion uses
\begin{equation}
 P_{t+1}(j)\propto P_t(j)\max\{L_t(j),\eps\}^{\beta_t},
 \label{eq:tgcdi-update}
\end{equation}
with the explicit quality/channel/OOD gate described above, rather than a joint-posterior or direct trust-exponent update. Calibration-only actions set the structural exponent to zero while updating supported metrology state. An accepted packet is therefore not automatically structural evidence.

\subsection{Joint Geometry--Waveform and Horizon Search}
Source position, receiver position, band, amplitude, duration, and waveform are selected from finite candidates under hard limits. The library supports impulse, sine, chirp, tone burst, Ricker, multisine, phase-coded, complementary-coded, and spectrally notched excitation. Generated waveforms are bounded after modulation/coding. Frequency, amplitude, duration, spacing, no-go, and availability violations receive machine-readable reasons. Only cached analytical level 0 and physics-signature level 1 are live; neither is a validated FE or learned-surrogate adapter. The fast beam reranker scores geometric routes without observation or belief rollouts, with a configured nominal horizon of up to three. Actual observation branching belongs to the optional physical-loss controller and has horizon at most two. Neither search is globally optimal.

\subsection{Discrepancy, OOD, and Decision Gating}
An online ridge discrepancy model learns residual feature correction between predicted and observed responses and converts residual error to model trust $\rho\in[0,1]$. Robust residual distance, an ensemble disagreement score, and a conformal tail score are combined for OOD monitoring; the conformal component remains withheld until ten reference scores exist. Reported decision confidence is capped by OOD evidence. The stopping controller considers confidence, normalized entropy, credible-region area, Bayes risk, expected value of another measurement, budget, and model trust. It may continue, verify, escalate to a reference method, or abstain. These mechanisms reduce unsupported certainty but do not guarantee statistical calibration under physical domain shift.

\subsection{Evidence, Replay, and Distributed Operation}
Every state-changing event can be recorded as canonical JSON with timestamp, prior hash, and SHA-256 entry hash. Exported ZIP bundles contain a manifest, session state, ledger, responses, planner explanations, configuration, versions, and checksums; import verifies integrity before accepting the bundle. Replay readers support NPZ, CSV, and WAV response banks, while hidden truth remains sealed until the policy has stopped. SQLite-backed research jobs provide progress, cancellation, and interrupted-job recovery for counterfactual-bank generation, calibration, benchmarks, ablations, and surrogate studies. A phone progressive web application supplies camera overlay, inertial-quality metadata, and microphone acquisition; a reconnecting laptop edge client can register, heartbeat, poll, and return microphone measurements over a private LAN. These paths remain operator-supervised research tooling.

\subsection{ARGUS-X Runtime Assurance and Failure Disposition}
The extended interface retains normalized heuristic screening weights under legacy healthy/no-detectable-damage, known-damage-candidate, and unknown/unsupported labels. Let $p_d=\sigma(\ell_d)$ summarize residual-SNR evidence that passed structural gates; unsupported weight uses $\max(o,1-\rho)$ before renormalization. These values are not fitted class probabilities, defect-existence inference, or POD estimates. Spatial modes are competing single-scatterer explanations. The legacy defect-count display is an ambiguity proxy, not inference of zero, one, or several actual defects.

Each sensor channel maintains a beta-form quality-weighted health score
\begin{equation}
\hat r_j=\frac{\alpha_j}{\alpha_j+\beta_j},
\end{equation}
where accepted observations add soft quality evidence and rejections add failure evidence. This score is not a calibrated sensor-failure probability. Channel health is evaluated before structural or calibration updates; an accepted waveform from a still-unreliable channel cannot update either state. Degraded channels temper evidence and request redundancy; unreliable channels force abstention. Environment metadata provides drift warnings. Duplicate identifiers/payloads, unsupported units/bandwidth, invalid samples/windows, clipping, silence, and infeasible actions are rejected. Timestamp skew is a warning rather than independently measured synchronization evidence.

A persisted emergency latch blocks acquisition until a human supplies an acknowledgment and reason; both transitions are audited. ARGUS may continue, reacquire/repair, mark a candidate region for reference inspection, or require a human. It cannot release a structure as healthy: a low residual-screening index instead requests independent reference verification.

The earlier 1,331-item ARGUS-X register recorded 277 narrow controls as tested, 588 partially mitigated, 244 requiring physical validation, 45 requiring literature/legal review, and 177 as bounded. These historical dispositions are not 1,331 solved claims or current-suite verification counts; the dated release report records the October checks.

\section{Learned Forward Surrogate and Data}
\subsection{Domain-Randomized Synthetic Set}
The optional learned component approximates expected response features, not the final posterior. Each row contains 24 inputs describing defect state, panel/material nuisance variables, and experiment parameters, and 15 outputs from Table~\ref{tab:features}. Three thousand examples were generated with locations in $[0.08,0.92]^2$, radii 0.04--0.14, severity 0.35--0.96, all four defect types, panel sizes 0.45--0.85 by 0.30--0.60 m, velocity 145--245 m/s, attenuation 1.0--2.4 m$^{-1}$, resonance 2.5--4.2 kHz, damping 70--135 s$^{-1}$, noise 0.003--0.014, and randomized probes, bands, amplitudes, and waveforms.

The PyTorch \cite{paszke2019} network is
\begin{equation}
 24\rightarrow96\rightarrow96\rightarrow15,
\end{equation}
with SiLU activations and LayerNorm after the first hidden layer. Inputs and targets are standardized on training statistics. The historical training used Smooth L1 loss, AdamW at learning rate $1.8\times10^{-3}$ and weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch size 128, a seeded 70/15/15 split, and early stopping. Not all randomized damping, noise, and system-delay quantities appear in the 24 inputs, so the predictor must average over unobserved variability. The surrogate remains outside the live planner.

\subsection{Measured LMSD Adapter}
The downloader queries the official Zenodo record, downloads declared NPZ files, and verifies each published MD5 checksum. The adapter converts each complex 0--1600 Hz FRF to a 0.12 s impulse response, normalizes known node coordinates, encodes the added mass as a dense inclusion, and extracts the same feature contract. Six scenarios times seven sources times seven receivers yield 294 channels. To prevent leakage, splitting is by entire damage scenario: 196 training, 49 validation, and 49 test channels. These channels are correlated views of six physical damage configurations, not 294 independent defects.

\subsection{Measured Acoustic and Paired Localization Bridges}
A second checksum-verifying adapter processes the TU Darmstadt GFRP acoustic dataset \cite{feldmann2026dataset}: 2,568 unaugmented microphone taps from eight 360 by 280 mm plates, including 2,100 intact and 468 defect-labelled point measurements. Signals are rationally resampled from 51.2 to 16 kHz and converted by the production feature contract. Splitting holds out complete physical plates. Separate healthy/damage robust CORAL maps align synthetic and measured first/second feature moments, while a robust empirical reference uses only gain-insensitive features. For external physical acquisitions, only the upper 10\% measured-reference tail contributes to OOD caution or abstention; simulation sessions are unaffected.

The Bologna acoustic-emission record \cite{zonzini2024dataset} adds 1,000 ray-traced Tx--Rx paths with fourth-order boundary reflections and 27 measured channels from nine known impact positions on a $1\times1\times0.003$ m aluminium plate. Its 2 MHz, 250 kHz domain is not mixed with the low-frequency GFRP waveforms. It instead supports a separate simulation-transfer timing benchmark and a measured few-shot localization calibration. Repository files are accepted only after publisher-declared size and MD5 verification, and manifests preserve record, DOI, license, and selected-file provenance.

\section{Physical and Software Realization}
\subsection{Acquisition Abstraction}
All devices implement connect, disconnect, acquire, and status operations. \code{SimulationDevice} executes Eq.~\eqref{eq:forward}; \code{MicrophoneDevice} records through the local audio stack; \code{UploadedSignalDevice} accepts WAV files; and \code{SerialProbeDevice} performs a line-framed transaction with an ESP32. Missing audio or serial hardware is reported without disabling simulation.

Uploads are limited to 10 MB, checked for allowed media types, decoded in memory, and explicitly downmixed to mono. Finite recordings must cover the declared experiment window and support the excitation band at their original sampling rate before polyphase resampling. Longer windows are cropped with recorded provenance; shorter recordings are rejected without padding missing evidence. Physical-unit inputs require declared conversion to the normalized inference contract. The upload API does not accept arbitrary filesystem paths. Simulation truth is omitted from ordinary payloads until reveal.

\subsection{Calibration and Coordinates}
The simulation calibration routine generates three healthy-reference acquisitions and records supported timing/velocity, noise, gain, and baseline-RMS diagnostics; it does not estimate resonance. Physical calibration requires acquired reference evidence. The session controller may request direct-path, coupling-repeat, phone-pose, microphone-level, or frequency-sweep actions. Each update requires its measured diagnostics; absolute pose requires an external pose reference, and waveform quality does not identify separate transmitter/receiver coupling. Supported metrology changes are persisted. Normalized coordinates provide a common interface, while predictions and reported millimetres use actual panel dimensions through Eq.~\eqref{eq:physical-map}.

A camera-guidance mode asks the operator to click the four panel corners in top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left order. Eight homography parameters are solved from the four point correspondences \cite{hartley2004}. The browser overlays source, receiver, propagation path, posterior field, suspected region, and a 90\% covariance ellipse. Visual-position error and inertial deviation are sent as quality metadata and reduce evidence weight; this remains approximate operator guidance, not certified optical metrology.

\subsection{ESP32 Probe}
The current classic-ESP32 reference uses DAC GPIO25, conditioned ADC GPIO34, GPIO26 as an excitation timing marker, and serial at at least 115200 baud for supported acquisition. Its version-2 experiment-bound protocol retains concurrent drive/capture, pretrigger samples, actual call timestamps, DAC codes and complete-frame status, as specified in the later acquisition section. Legacy excite-then-record firmware is refused by arrival inference. The DAC must feed an appropriate AC-coupled amplifier rather than power the exciter directly. The receiver needs input protection, bias, buffering and antialias conditioning. Electrical timing and transfer remain unmeasured.

\subsection{Persistence and Reproducibility}
Additive SQLite migrations preserve legacy sessions while storing separate inference blocks, probes, events, jobs, model records, and hash-linked ledger entries, alongside configuration, calibration, compressed responses, features, scores, and posterior history. NumPy/SciPy provide vectorized numerics and signal processing \cite{harris2020,virtanen2020}. Fixed seeds cover defect generation, observation noise, splits, training, and paired policy comparison. Full-precision structural state retains small supported hypotheses; rejected observations do not become accepted repeat references after hydration. Source fingerprints and saved outputs permit checks against the actual changed source, rather than only a repository commit identifier.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
The evaluation addresses five questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1:} Does active planning concentrate spatial belief faster than random or uniform probing?
    \item \textbf{RQ2:} Does lower uncertainty translate into lower localization error under the same budget?
    \item \textbf{RQ3:} Can a small learned surrogate reproduce domain-randomized signal features?
    \item \textbf{RQ4:} Do those learned features transfer across held-out physical damage scenarios?
    \item \textbf{RQ5:} What experiment would be required to establish the proposed TG-CDI technical effect?
\end{itemize}

For RQ1--RQ2, 30 medium-preset defects were generated with seeds 100--129. Each of three policies operated on the identical hidden defect for a maximum of ten measurements. \emph{Random} sampled continuous source and receiver positions and a frequency band. \emph{Uniform grid} followed a fixed geometry sequence. \emph{ARGUS} used Eq.~\eqref{eq:planner}. Policies were compared using mean and median localization error, normalized final entropy, entropy reduction, entropy area under the measurement curve (AUC), physical cost, experiment count, and success within 10, 15, 20, and 30 mm. Paired bootstrap 95\% intervals were computed over the shared cases. No assertion required error to improve monotonically.

For RQ3, the 3000-example synthetic model used seed 23 and independent random splitting. For RQ4, the LMSD model used the same architecture and seed but held out complete scenarios. Smooth L1 values across these two data regimes are not directly comparable because their target statistics and split difficulty differ.

RQ5 is a prospective, preregistered test rather than a result in this paper. On a complete physical response bank, compare TG-CDI against raster coverage, a uniform sequence, random selection, fixed-network reconstruction, entropy-only active selection, calibration-disabled TG-CDI, trust-disabled TG-CDI, and worst-rival-disabled TG-CDI. Freeze losses and stopping thresholds before revealing blind truth. Primary endpoints should be measurements and physical cost to reach a fixed decision-risk threshold; secondary endpoints should include localization error, probability of detection with confidence bounds, false-stop rate, abstention rate, calibration coverage, remount repeatability, temperature-shift robustness, and wall-clock time. The differentiating hypothesis survives only if the full policy improves the prespecified primary endpoint without an unacceptable false-negative or unsupported-stop penalty.

\section{Technical Differentiation and Candidate Protectable Nucleus}
The system-level research hypothesis is that a controller able to improve both object and instrument estimates may use a measurement budget more effectively. This effect remains unproven. Seven implemented aspects are relevant to testing that hypothesis:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Live rival construction:} separated modes are extracted from the current posterior, so the next experiment targets the ambiguity that actually remains.
    \item \textbf{Joint actionable uncertainty:} structural and metrology marginals determine the next action, rather than being passive confidence displays.
    \item \textbf{Action arbitration:} the fast policy uses declared switching heuristics; the optional physical-loss policy compares reference calibration and diagnosis under one modeled localization loss.
    \item \textbf{Finite candidate selection:} geometry/frequency predictions discriminate represented rivals, while waveform, amplitude, duration, and costs also influence heuristic ranking. The two live feature-model levels and the optional scalar-arrival planner have separate approximation boundaries.
    \item \textbf{Trust-gated evidence:} quality, channel reliability, model discrepancy, and OOD evidence control the strength of the physical likelihood instead of merely changing a displayed confidence score.
    \item \textbf{Constraint-aware execution:} infeasible frequency, amplitude, spacing, no-go, and unavailable-device actions are rejected and explained before execution.
    \item \textbf{End-to-end auditability:} decomposed alternatives, deterministic explanations, raw-response checksums, posterior evolution, model identity, and decisions form a verifiable evidence chain.
\end{enumerate}

The candidate subject for professional examination is an apparatus-limited acoustic sequence that maintains a location belief and correlated timing/velocity metrology block, compares direct-path calibration with diagnostic bistatic actions under a common modeled localization loss including a subsequent diagnostic observation, executes one feasible finite-bank action, gates measured evidence before structural fusion, and requires fresh consistent verification for a confident stop. It also records action, observation, rationale, posterior transition, and model identity. This supported sequence is narrower than a general joint posterior, hardest-rival optimal policy, arbitrary waveform design, or multi-fidelity physical solver. Neither the established mathematical components nor their combination are presumed patentable.

This positioning does not establish novelty or non-obviousness. The reviewed references already cover important subsets, and additional patents may be closer. Commercially, TG-CDI is best positioned as adaptive inspection-orchestration middleware for low-channel, movable-probe, measurement-budget-limited, or access-constrained workflows. It could complement a robot, phased-array instrument, guided-wave tool, permanent SHM network, or acoustic-emission service rather than claim to replace their qualified acquisition hardware. The sellable proposition is a measurable reduction in inspection actions or decision cost at matched detection/false-alarm risk, plus calibrated abstention and replayable evidence. \section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\subsection{Model and Inference Limits}
The simulator is nondispersive and uses one effective velocity. A real composite plate supports multiple dispersive, anisotropic modes and boundary reflections. The location grid is conditional on one dominant scatterer; size, severity, and type remain unchanged priors, and healthy/existence/count reconstruction is absent. Baseline subtraction assumes sufficient nominal-twin calibration. Successive generalized likelihood multiplication approximates conditional independence; shared error can still over-concentrate the belief. The correlated particle helper is not a deployed hierarchical inference engine.

Confidence in Eq.~\eqref{eq:confidence} is a heuristic mixture, not a calibrated posterior coverage probability. The historical ECE compared that proxy with a different event and has been deprecated; a corrected event-matched reliability calculation still requires independent physical validation. The planner utilities and cost conversion were engineered rather than elicited from a certified maintenance process. The conformal tail is withheld before ten scores and is only meaningful if future calibration scores are exchangeable with deployment. The block-structured state, locally Gaussian propagation covariance, compact counterfactual features, online ridge correction, and bounded lookahead are deliberate approximations.

\subsection{Evaluation Limits}
Thirty baseline synthetic cases provide a useful paired study, not population-scale validation. The baseline mean-error intervals cross zero; no claim of superior physical localization is warranted. The expanded NEO matrix has only two cases and is exclusively a software/research smoke test. Baselines do not include every advanced tomography, Gaussian-process design, reinforcement-learning, or optimal-array method. Hyperparameters were not evaluated in a nested holdout, and most studies share simulator assumptions with inference.

The LMSD experiment has only six independent damage scenarios, several with multiple masses. Hammer/accelerometer FRFs at 0--1600 Hz differ from the prototype's 1.2--6.2 kHz chirp interrogation. Added masses are scattering surrogates, not cavities or delaminations. Scenario-held-out performance correctly reveals that correlated channel count cannot replace independent physical diversity.

\subsection{Operational Limits}
Microphone measurements are vulnerable to airborne sound and ambient noise. Probe remounting changes coupling. Camera homography assumes a planar stationary panel and accurate clicks; inertial/visual metadata are quality proxies, not traceable metrology. Clock-skew checks, heartbeats and reconnect logic do not provide hard real-time synchronization. The v2 ESP32 scheduling marker and ADC call timestamps require external electrical timing/transfer validation; analog conditioning and physical power limits are still apparatus requirements. Unaligned physical recordings carry zero inference weight. The prototype is not certified NDE equipment and must not be used as the sole basis for safety-critical maintenance decisions.

\section{Physical Validation and Data-Collection Plan}
\label{sec:physical-validation}
A credible next study should use at least three nominally identical panels, eight repeatably mounted perimeter transducers, an instrumented exciter, five repeats per condition, and healthy measurements before and after each damage block. Reversible bonded masses, damping patches, or paired magnets are appropriate for commissioning. True cavities or delaminations require manufactured specimens---for example flat-bottom holes or embedded release films---and independent truth from ultrasound, radiography, or destructive sectioning where justified.

For the current acquisition contract, collect all 56 ordered non-self source--receiver pairs, three frequency bands, and five repeats. One physical state yields $56\times3\times5=840$ waveforms. Twenty known locations plus healthy references yield approximately 17,000 signals. Preserve raw waveforms and record specimen, material, thickness, defect geometry and truth method, source and receiver coordinates, excitation, repeat, temperature, humidity, coupling force, remount identifier, calibration, timestamp, and code version.

Split development data by complete defect location, stronger validation by complete specimen, and the final test by a blind panel whose truth remains hidden until predictions and stopping decisions are frozen. Offline planner evaluation additionally needs a complete counterfactual bank: every admissible experiment must be recorded for every hidden state so all policies query the same physical response table. Report error versus measurement budget, entropy calibration, success radii, abstention, remount robustness, and temperature shift.

\section{Conclusion}
ARGUS NEO demonstrates an inspectable software loop for selecting the next acoustic experiment and checking the reliability of its evidence. It combines location and metrology blocks, finite diagnostic/reference selection, declared verification/abstention rules, and gated structural fusion. The optional controller evaluates reference calibration and diagnosis using at most two arrival-observation branches and a common physical localization loss. ARGUS-X preserves heuristic unsupported-state indicators, rejects duplicate or infeasible evidence, and provides a human-controlled emergency latch and replayable integrity record; it does not establish a healthy structural release. Historical matched-model results support faster modeled uncertainty concentration than simple policies, while localization-error superiority remains unproven. October controls improve specified software invariants without establishing inspection efficacy. A candidate practical use is orchestration for constrained or sparse sensing, contingent on measured benefit. The required next evidence is a preregistered, specimen-held-out blind physical campaign with complete counterfactual coverage, independent truth, calibrated coverage, predefined losses, and component ablations. Technical novelty, human inventorship, ownership, and filing eligibility require separate professional review.

\section{Interpretation Guide for Demonstrations}
An entropy decrease means probability mass has concentrated, not necessarily that the mode is correct. Confidence combines concentration and local mass and is OOD-capped, but it is not a certified probability of correctness. Ground truth must remain hidden until the controller stops. Reveal physical error using panel dimensions, not normalized distance. If entropy falls while revealed error remains high, diagnose mismatch or overconfidence rather than calling the run successful. Never describe a two-case matrix, one controlled mismatch case, or simulated calibration coverage as general performance.

A concise description for a judge is: ``ARGUS is a research prototype that uses predicted responses of competing location hypotheses to select the next acoustic experiment, and can request calibration or withhold an unsupported decision. It records the action, measurement and posterior update for inspection.'' A defensible evidence statement is: ``The dated release-readiness report records the completed software checks. Historical synthetic risk-controller results are mixed, and the controller remains optional. Physical accuracy and patentability remain unestablished.''
